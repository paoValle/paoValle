## About

Curiosity has always been my compass. Technology is not a job I picked — it is something I have
been doing since I was a kid, pushing myself to build my own projects: proofs of concept,
experiments, small tools for problems I had. Most of them were useless. All of them taught me
something, and none of them were homework.

I never waited for a course to teach me. Straight out of high school I started writing web
software for real clients: two management systems — one in pharmaceutical manufacturing and
medical sales rep management, one for surgical practices — in PHP, HTML, JS and CSS, which at
the beginning I barely knew. I learned by breaking them and fixing them, on the job, and **they
are still in production today**. When AI-assisted coding arrived, I finally had the chance to
restructure and refactor them into something I would write today.

At university I built **OrariUnina**: it collected the lecture timetable of every course and
degree programme and derived **which classrooms were free at any given time**, so students
could find a place to study.

I think like a product owner before I think like an engineer: first the person using it and the
problem, then the feature, then the code. Today my focus is **LLMs, RAG architectures and
agentic AI** in enterprise systems, and in the last year I have followed the field without
pause — models, releases, benchmarks, evaluation practices, and the people worth following.
